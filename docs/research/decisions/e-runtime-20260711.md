## Invariant assessment

The direct and direct-journal controls emitted two same-key effects in all
three repetitions. Keyed and mailbox rows emitted one while the unrelated peer
completed through the selected executor while coordinated work was held. Every
started local child is retained by PID; shutdown lists no survivors, including
both same-key children in the direct control.

The disposable coordinator wrote a fsynced claim, was killed with `SIGKILL`,
and did not release its child itself. A separately launched recovery coordinator
read that claim, fenced the still-live stale process group, rejected the stale
generation, advanced to generation 2, reacquired a fresh child, and stopped it.
This proves the harness recovery sequence, not a daemon recovery contract.

The Compose lane killed each holder client, then explicitly terminated the
identified detached PostgreSQL backend when the lock remained held. Only after
that observed connection loss did a new session reacquire generation 2. The
stale-generation insert was empty and the fresh insert succeeded. This is a
real disposable database/session fault, but it cannot fence an already-issued
external process effect.

## Comparison and limits

Keyed guards and mailboxes remain bounded intra-process candidates only. The
journal is an audit record, not a fence: direct journal controls remain unsafe.
The measured 14.0–14.5 ms positive peer completions are harness timing, not
daemon throughput, player impact, or executor-selection proof.

No candidate has real daemon transaction ordering, cancellation, queue bounds,
Kubernetes effects, or reconciliation. Kubernetes effect, observation, and
recovery probes are `BLOCKED`, not passed by the planner tests. Cross-daemon
external-effect fencing is also unproved despite the PostgreSQL session result.

## Reconsideration conditions

A synthesis task may reconsider only after an independent reproduction checks a
real daemon's durable intent, generation, effect, and observation ordering. It
must cover process and database connection loss, cancellation, queue bounds,
restart between every boundary, and a legal disposable Kubernetes cluster. Any
unavailable probe must remain `BLOCKED` or partial.

## Follow-up boundary

claim is created here. Retain the E-RUNTIME branch and raw root only for review
and explicit cleanup. The next permitted step is synthesis across independently
