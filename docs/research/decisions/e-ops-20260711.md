# E-OPS research decision 2026-07-11

## Purpose

Dispose of the operations experiment without adopting a runtime, release, or
external-service claim.

## Disposition

`reject` the combined candidate for adoption now. Keep the Compose and restore
observations as research evidence only. The local immutable-toolchain finding
fails the candidate, while rootless Docker, Kubernetes, and signing remain
external proof pending rather than passed.

## Evidence and invariants

The evidence is the [hypothesis](../experiments/e-ops-20260711.md) and the
[E-OPS run](../runs/e-ops-20260711.md). The runner at
`3446580f2b5634a03328697c5486b06c2b4d7e73` retained redacted ignored output
and removed its unique Compose resources. It did not alter product source,
owner contracts, controller state, or state matrices.

The rebuilt Compose gate, PostgreSQL dump/restore/daemon `doctor`, two matching
release-daemon hashes, test-only fault boundary inspection, and a constrained
user systemd scope passed. They do not prove player traffic, a support bundle,
a production systemd unit, rootless execution, cluster behavior, or signing.

## Reason and combinations

A source tag is not immutable acquisition evidence. The Dockerfile lacks a
pinned base-image digest and Rustup checksum; the Gradle wrapper lacks its
distribution checksum. Adopting a release or supply-chain statement on these
facts would be false.

The Compose and restore candidates can combine with a later immutable-toolchain
change, independently reviewed rootless engine, and a dedicated support-bundle
and loaded fault scenario. The systemd observation is useful only as a narrow
sandbox candidate; it must not replace a service-unit design. Kubernetes needs
a client, authorized disposable namespace, config, database URL, and cluster
credentials. Signing needs a trusted key and a signed source or artifact.

## Reconsideration and follow-up

Reconsider only after all acquisition checks are immutable, a fresh clean
Compose run succeeds, and each external lane records its guarded command and
observed effect. A fault/support candidate also needs a real bounded workload,
redacted generated support artifact, and negative secret scan.
